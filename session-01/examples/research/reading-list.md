# Early Lingnan (Canton Christian College), 1888–1904 — what was taught

Reading list compiled 6 November 2026 for the question: *what does scholarship say
about the curriculum and teaching of the early college?*

Searches were run under the institution's successive names — **Canton Christian
College · Christian College in China · Lingnan College · Lingnan University ·
格致書院 · 嶺南學堂 · 岭南学堂 · 嶺南大學** — in OpenAlex, Crossref, OpenAIRE,
Internet Archive, Open Library and Unpaywall.

> **Bottom line.** Modern scholarship does not describe the 1888–1904 curriculum
> in detail. The clearest course list comes from a 1954 memoir by a pupil who
> entered in 1902, corroborated in outline by Corbett 1963 and by a 1903
> girls'-school account. Liu Bao-zhe 2013 is the one article squarely on the
> Macao years; Dong Wang 2007 is the monographic treatment, but I have not read
> it. The detailed curriculum evidence is in section C.

Annotations state exactly what I opened. **"Record opened"** = title, abstract
and/or full text; **"record only"** = bibliographic metadata only. Nothing is
described from memory.

## A. Core scholarship

### 1. Dong Wang, *Managing God's Higher Learning: U.S.–China Cultural Encounter and Canton Christian College (Lingnan University), 1888–1952*
- Lanham, MD: Lexington Books, 2007. ISBN 978-0-7391-1935-8 / 978-0-7391-1936-5.
- Links: <https://openlibrary.org/works/OL9455024W> · lending copy <https://archive.org/details/managinggodshigh0000dong>
- **Status:** record opened (Internet Archive metadata; SHAFR guide entry listing the chapter structure). Text **not read** — controlled digital lending, and the full-text search endpoint returned 403.
- The only book-length study of the college across 1888–1952. The chapter list given by the SHAFR guide entry: the setting (Honglok, Guangzhou); cultural migration — Lingnan as foreign and local institution; financing and governance; "The Advance to Higher Education: Women's Education, Power and Modernization"; Charles K. Edmunds and the move to Pomona. The early curriculum is presumably handled in chapters 1–4, but **I have not verified what the book says**.

### 2. Liu Bao-zhe, "A Study of Canton Christian College in Macao (from 1900 to 1904)"
- *Journal of Wuyi University* 15, no. 4 (2013): 59–63. No DOI.
- Link: <https://openalex.org/W821283263>
- **Status:** abstract opened (OpenAlex), in Chinese. Full text not accessed.
- The only article found squarely inside the 1888–1904 window. Abstract: Lingnan's predecessor was 格致書院; it operated in Macao for four years, was renamed 嶺南學堂 there, and moved back to Guangzhou in 1904; the Macao phase was the hinge in the passage from 學堂 to university, and a Western-style school that also affected Macao's education. It promises institutional and personnel detail, not a syllabus.

### 3. Charles Hodge Corbett, *Lingnan University: A Short History Based Primarily on the Records of the University's American Trustees*
- Trustees of Lingnan University, 1963.
- Links: <https://openlibrary.org/works/OL7375520W> · lending copy <https://archive.org/details/lingnanuniversit0000corb>
- **Status:** excerpts read via transcriptions already in this workspace (pp. 15, 16, 31, 34, 35); full text not read.
- The standard institutional history, written from the American trustees' records. The excerpts give the 1888 English-first policy, Chinese classics, morning prayers and Sunday Bible class, the "mongrel English" teaching problem, and physical training at Macao.

### 4. Gordon E. Slethaug, "Cross-cultural team teaching in China: A retrospective view"
- 2011, pp. 105–121 (book chapter; OpenAlex records DOI 10.4324/9780203842775-14, which Crossref returns 404).
- Link: <https://openalex.org/W2743696148>
- **Status:** abstract opened. Not a curriculum study.
- Opening historical framing: B. C. Henry and Andrew Happer moved to Guangzhou in 1885; the American Presbyterian Mission established the college in 1888; in 1893 the New York trustees obtained a charter from the Regents of the University of the State of New York.

### 5. Dong Wang, "Circulating American Higher Education: The Case of Lingnan University, 1888–1951"
- *Journal of American-East Asian Relations* (2000): 147–167. DOI 10.1163/187656100793645895.
- **Status:** abstract opened.
- Frames the college as a case of transferring American higher-education models. The abstract is mostly about the 1987 re-establishment and alumni philanthropy; do not expect a curriculum inventory.

### 6. Leping Mou, "The Liberal Arts Curriculum in China's Christian Universities and Its Relevance to China's Universities Today"
- PhD dissertation, University of Toronto, 2018. <https://hdl.handle.net/1807/89591>
- **Status:** record opened; snippets only. Not a study of the early period.
- Tabulates the curriculum of Lingnan and other Christian universities for 1919, 1937–38 and after. Useful as a model for how to reconstruct a course list, and for showing how far the later curriculum had moved from the 1902 list.

### 7. Yinrui Xie and Paul Walker, "Planning a Christian campus in Quasi-colonial China: Lingnan University, Guangzhou, 1904–1931"
- *Planning Perspectives* 37, no. 4 (2021): 685–712. DOI 10.1080/02665433.2021.1983857.
- **Status:** abstract opened (green OA record on figshare; no deposited file).
- Campus planning after the return to Guangzhou. Not about teaching.

### 8. Yinrui Xie, "Lingnan University, Guangzhou"
- In *Design as Communication* (Routledge, 2025), 112–141. DOI 10.4324/9781003563068-6.
- **Status:** record only. Design/architectural treatment.

## C. Primary sources — where the curriculum detail actually is

### 12. 敖士洲, 《五十年前之嶺南》 ("Lingnan Fifty Years Ago")
- 1954, manuscript, p. 2.
- **Status:** page transcription and one column crop (`sources/raw/memoir-p2-col12.png`) read.
- The fullest course list found: Bible, English, Chinese, geography, history, arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, physics, chemistry, zoology, botany, physiology, drawing, physical exercise; most subjects in English, Chinese taught by 鍾榮光; four-year 廣學班 later renamed 大學預科. The author entered in 1902.
- **Caution:** written fifty years later; OCR faulty; describes the preparatory department, not necessarily the whole college. Full quotation and caveats in `notes/early-lingnan-curriculum-evidence.md`.

### 13. *Canton Christian College: Its Field and Work in China*
- 1908, printed p. 9 (scan p. 12).
- **Status:** page image checked (`sources/raw/ccc-1908-p12.png`).
- Institutional chronology: 1894 purchase of the Pati School; 1898 relinquishment; the "new organization" opened 3 June 1899 with six students; June 1900 move to Macao; 1904 removal to its "present location"; enrolment 70 at Macao, 76 at Canton in 1905, 103 in 1906, 185 in 1907. No course list on this page.

### 14. Fanny Sinclair Woods et al., *The Beginnings of the Women's Department of the Canton Christian College*
- New York: Trustees of the Canton Christian College, 1915.
- Full text: <https://archive.org/details/beginingsofwomen00cant>
- **Status:** full text opened.
- "The Girls' School in 1903" (reprinted from *The Collegian*, July 1904) describes a school explicitly modelled on the boys' college: Bible, prayer and singing; three hours of English daily; arithmetic and Bible; geography and writing; Chinese studies in the afternoons. A pupil: "Every day we studied Chinese three hours and English three hours." (pp. 4–5, 10)

### 15. "The Canton Christian College"
- *Teachers College Record* 16, no. 2 (1915): 1–11. DOI 10.1177/016146811501600205.
- **Status:** record only; closed access.

### 16. "What China Thinks of Canton Christian College"
- *Teachers College Record* 18, no. 4 (1917): 1–6. DOI 10.1177/016146811701800407.
- **Status:** record only; closed access.

### 17. Philip N. Youtz, "The American College at Canton, China"
- *Current History* 22, no. 4 (1925): 604–608. DOI 10.1525/curh.1925.22.4.604.
- **Status:** record only.

### 18. China Educational Commission, *Christian Education in China: The Report of the China Educational Commission of 1921–1922*
- Shanghai: Commercial Press, 1922. <https://archive.org/details/MN41995ucmf_4>
- **Status:** full text opened. §656 recommends junior- and senior-college courses in education, agriculture, and perhaps commerce and social science. A 1922 recommendation, not a description of early teaching.

### 19. Charles K. Edmunds, *Canton Christian College Ling Naam Hok Hau: Its Growth and Outlook*
- Canton, 1919. <https://commons.ln.edu.hk/lingnan_history_bks/8>
- **Status:** record opened; PDF blocked by a Cloudflare challenge. Abstract: the 1918–19 president's report, with a brief history "from the beginning".

### 20. 嶺南大學, 《廣州嶺南大學》 / *Canton Christian College, Canton China*
- Guangzhou, c. 1920. <https://commons.ln.edu.hk/lingnan_history_bks/10>

## Gaps and next steps

1. **No modern study is devoted to the 1888–1904 curriculum.** The nearest are Liu 2013 (Macao 1900–1904) and the early chapters of Dong Wang 2007 / Corbett 1963.
2. **The best course list is a 1954 memoir**, not a work of scholarship. It needs to be read against the college's annual catalogues before being treated as settled.
3. **Chinese-language journal literature is effectively invisible** to OpenAlex, Crossref and OpenAIRE. Liu 2013 was found only because OpenAlex indexed its English title. CNKI / Wanfang / Baidu Scholar must be searched by hand (queries below).
4. **Archives.** The 吴梓明 bibliography (item 10) is the finding aid. The American trustees' records (Presbyterian Historical Society, Yale Divinity Library, Lingnan Foundation) are where the annual reports and catalogues cited by Corbett and Wang come from.
5. **Verify before citing.** Dong Wang's chapters 1–4 and Corbett's full text were not read here.
