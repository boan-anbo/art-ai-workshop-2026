# hall-of-quiet-study.jpg

File: `my-work/sources/raw/hall-of-quiet-study.jpg` — 600 × 446 px JPEG. Painting sits inside a cream paper margin, with a thin painted border line around the picture.

## Subject

An elevated view from a verandah into a three-bay Chinese interior, arranged symmetrically around an open doorway.

- **Foreground:** a dark turned-wood balustrade runs across the bottom. Behind it, a row of about seven potted plants stands on a tiled walkway: wide-mouthed green celadon bowls, blue-glazed planters, and a large green jar on a blue-and-white stand. Most hold clumps of narrow, grass-like leaves; two carry bright pink curled blooms; the two end pots hold taller woody plants with small round orange fruit.
- **Doors and windows:** tall green-lacquered folding doors, four leaves thrown open at the centre and further sets left and right. The flanking windows are glazed in a grid of small panes; through them, from this height, one sees a neighbouring building's timber balcony and terracotta-tiled roofs. A wooden staircase with a curved handrail drops away at the right, hung with a potted plant.
- **Left room:** a long bamboo-coloured couch under the window, small tables, and a stepped display shelf holding framed panels and plaques. A man in a blue gown sits in profile at a desk, writing or reading with a brush.
- **Central room:** the axis of the picture. A raised dais or kang with a green cover and bolster; two drum stools in front; a striped mat or board laid on the floor; a long dark cabinet behind with red objects on it; hanging scrolls with pink-red borders on the back wall.
- **Centre, near the threshold:** a second man in a blue gown lounges on a low daybed behind a desk, head propped on one hand. A third figure, also in blue, sits at a table to the right holding a book.
- **Right room:** dark green walls hung with framed pictures and a large tilted mirror; a marble-topped table carrying glass bell jars, a clock and small boxes.
- **Ceiling:** decorated with painted leaf and scroll patterns; two square painted lanterns hang just inside the doors.

## Style and medium

Watercolour with opaque body colour (gouache) on paper — probably pith paper, though the surface is too small in this scan to confirm.

- Ink outline under flat, bright, evenly applied colour; almost no shading, no cast shadows, uniform light.
- Colour is dominated by jade green joinery, ultramarine blue furniture and inscriptions, and ochre floor tiles — a bright, decorative palette.
- Composition is a one-point perspective cutaway with a high viewpoint: the balustrade and plants frame a stage-like interior, everything parallel to the picture plane.
- Architectural detail is drawn with fine ruled lines and precise joinery; plants and figures are looser and more repetitive — the signature economy of workshop production.
- The frame is drawn, not carved: a painted border in the Canton export manner, notably the blue-green key-fret band across the top.

## Visible text

All inscriptions are white on blue boards, in regular script.

- **Plaque, top centre** (horizontal, four large characters, read right to left): 靜觀自得 — "quiet contemplation brings its own reward". To its left edge, two or three small vertical characters, apparently a signature or dedication; not legible at this resolution.
- **Couplet, left jamb of the far-left door**, vertical: 半夜書聲月在天 — "at midnight, the sound of reading; the moon in the sky".
- **Couplet, right jamb of the far-right window**, vertical: 一簾花影雲滿地 — "a curtain of flower shadows; clouds covering the ground". The fifth character is small and could read 雪 rather than 雲.

## Guesses

- **Canton (Guangzhou) export painting, c. 1830–1870.** Reason: green-lacquer joinery with glazed upper panels, glass bell jars and a marble-topped table alongside Chinese furniture, and the flat bright watercolour-on-paper technique are all standard products of the Canton painting workshops for Western buyers.
- **The room is a scholar's studio (書齋) in a wealthy merchant or official house.** Reason: the plaque and couplet are about study and reading, and the fittings — desk with brush, curio shelf, kang with scrolls, framed calligraphy — are a literatus interior rather than a reception hall.
- **The support is pith paper (通草紙).** Reason: the opaque, chalky look and absence of visible paper grain fit pith, the usual support for this export genre; needs raking light or a loupe to confirm.
- **The sheet came from an album or a dealer's mount.** Reason: the even cream margin of uniform width on all four sides, outside the painted border.
- **It belongs to a series of interior scenes rather than standing alone.** Reason: the stage-like, single-viewpoint format matches sets of "interior" paintings sold in albums of a hundred subjects; no series number is visible to confirm.
- **The living plants are kumquat (金桔) at the ends and cymbidium or sweet flag in the blue pots.** Reason: small round orange fruit on woody stems at the ends, and narrow tufted leaves elsewhere; the pink blooms are not identifiable at this size.
