# ju-lian-insects-and-flowers.jpg

File: `my-work/sources/raw/ju-lian-insects-and-flowers.jpg` — 599 × 537 px JPEG. Unframed sheet on a cream mount, square picture area.

## Subject

A spray of white blossom with six bees or wasps, painted against blank paper.

- **Branches:** two or three woody stems enter from the bottom edge and cross in an X in the left third of the sheet; the main stem runs up almost vertically through the centre. Bark is pale grey-brown with a dry, granular texture, and the stems are cut off by the sheet edge rather than rooted.
- **Flowers:** about five open blooms near the left edge and across the lower middle, plus a dozen round buds along the twigs. Five broad, rounded white petals, cream at the base, with a fan of fine stamens carrying yellow anthers. Buds are cream with brown-green sepals.
- **Leaves:** small, sparse, lance-shaped, in muted grey-green and pale blue-green — some lying almost flat and transparent-looking, others folded.
- **Insects:** six, all small striped bees or wasps, dispersed around the flowers rather than settled on them: one high at upper centre, one upper left, two in the centre-left below the crossing stem, one at mid-right in open space, and one on a flower at lower centre-right. Wings are drawn as pale grey membranes; abdomens are banded yellow and black.
- **Ground and setting:** none. The background is bare paper, slightly foxed and toned, empty across the whole upper right.

## Style and medium

Chinese ink and colour on paper, in the boneless (沒骨) manner — no outline encloses any petal or leaf.

- Petals are built from thin washes of white and cream pigment rather than line; the white sits opaquely on the surface in places.
- Leaves and buds show wet-in-wet pooling: pigment has drawn back to the edges leaving pale centres and a darker rim, the "settling water" (撞水) effect; the opaque white "settling powder" (撞粉) effect appears in the flower heads.
- Branches are brushed with a drier, scrubbier ink line over a pale wash, giving a matte, slightly rough bark.
- Palette is restrained and cool: ink grey, celadon and grey-green, cream, a little ochre, with the only saturated notes being the yellow-and-black insects.
- Composition is asymmetric and off-centre — the mass sits low and left, the upper right is left blank — the standard layout of a Chinese album leaf.
- Insect anatomy is drawn with fine, even, almost entomological line, in contrast with the soft washes of the flowers.

## Visible text

- No inscription or signature anywhere on the sheet; the upper right is empty.
- Two red seals only:
  - **Lower left**, about 3 cm square, thin red border with red seal-script characters (zhuwen), apparently two characters.
  - **Lower right**, slightly larger, a dense square seal with characters reversed out of a red ground, apparently four characters.
- Neither seal can be read at this resolution; no transcription is offered here.

## Guesses

- **By Ju Lian (居廉, 1828–1904) of Panyu, or his cousin Ju Chao (居巢, 1811–1865) — the "Two Ju".** Reason: the combination of boneless flowers with meticulously drawn insects, the muted celadon-and-cream palette and the visible settling-water and settling-powder effects are the signature of their workshop. The filename attributes it to Ju Lian, but that attribution comes from the filename, not from anything legible in the image.
- **The lower-left seal may read 居廉.** Reason: the left glyph has the 尸-over-古 structure of 居 and the right has a 广 roof consistent with 廉; at this resolution this is a resemblance, not a reading.
- **A white plum (梅) or pear (梨) branch.** Reason: five rounded white petals with a long stamen fan, round buds on short spurs, and small lance-shaped leaves; plum is the more common subject for this format, pear the closer match for the leaf shape.
- **An album leaf from a set of flower-and-insect studies (花鸟草虫册).** Reason: square format, no inscription, seals only at the two lower corners, and the subject cropped at the sheet edge — all typical of a leaf detached from an album.
- **Mid-to-late nineteenth century.** Reason: consistent with the working dates of the Two Ju and with the toned, foxed paper; nothing in the image dates it more precisely.
- **Sized paper (熟宣) rather than unsized.** Reason: the washes sit on the surface with hard edges and little bleeding, and the white pigment stays opaque instead of sinking in.
